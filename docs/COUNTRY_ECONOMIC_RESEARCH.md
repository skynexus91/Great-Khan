# Why The Great Khan? A country-by-country economic research brief

**Research edition:** October 2026 | **Language:** English | **Status:** exploratory evidence, not an investment prospectus.

## Historical context

Inflation, currency devaluation, conflict, financial exclusion, and disruptions to payment systems have repeatedly weakened households' ability to save, pay suppliers, and maintain everyday economic activity. High inflation is not the same as a payment outage: a digital token does not automatically protect purchasing power, stabilize prices, restore banking access, or resolve fiscal instability.

Historical precedents include Latin American currency crises, post-conflict monetary instability, the long-running inflation episodes in Zimbabwe and Venezuela, and more recent cost-of-living shocks. The Great Khan is motivated by the **hypothesis** that a low-cost, transparent, accessible payment and community-support infrastructure could help people and small businesses transact more reliably in some settings. This is a hypothesis to validate with users, not a demonstrated result.

## Country-level historical interpretation

### Latin America: Argentina, Bolivia, Venezuela
Episodes of rapid inflation and foreign-exchange restrictions can make prices, wages, and working capital difficult to plan. A research pilot should distinguish (a) currency risk, (b) remittance costs, (c) domestic merchant acceptance, and (d) banking access. A speculative token can add exchange-rate risk rather than remove it. Venezuela's displayed 2016 World Bank figure must **not** be presented as its current inflation.

### Europe and neighboring economies: Romania, Moldova, Ukraine, Türkiye
These economies differ greatly. Romania has mature regulated payment infrastructure; Ukraine faces war-related continuity challenges; Moldova has important cross-border payment use cases; Türkiye has experienced high inflation. A credible project should compare actual consumer fees and reliability against existing domestic and international alternatives instead of assuming a blockchain is better.

### Africa: Nigeria, Angola, Malawi, Zimbabwe, Sudan
The combination of inflation, informal trade, mobile connectivity constraints, and financial-access disparities makes affordability and offline usability important research topics. The World Bank's most recent Zimbabwe and Sudan values in this source are older observations, **not contemporaneous measurements**. Pilot selection requires up-to-date local legal, consumer-protection and infrastructure research.

### Middle East: Lebanon and West Bank and Gaza
Banking disruption, conflict, and inflation create very different risks. Payments technology alone cannot solve blocked supply chains, conflict, sanctions restrictions, liquidity shortages or humanitarian access. Research should prioritize safety, lawful access, consumer protection, and independent oversight.

## Why research The Great Khan?

The founding aspiration is not a promise of token appreciation. It is to explore whether people can sustain everyday economic exchange—buying bread, paying a small supplier, supporting community infrastructure—when conventional options are costly, unreliable or inaccessible.

A **testable product thesis** is a transparent, low-fee payment and community-support layer, initially only an educational simulation and Ethereum Sepolia prototype. Potential benefits to validate include clear transaction costs, traceable public decisions, resilient merchant workflows, multilingual mobile access and accountable community programs.

**No proof of necessity or superiority yet exists.** Real-world adoption requires interviews, controlled pilots, measured user outcomes and comparison with existing mobile money, banks, cash, regulated stablecoins and instant-payment systems.

## Design tensions and safeguards

1. **Inflation vs token volatility:** KHAN has no fiat peg, redemption guarantee, stable purchasing power or assured liquidity. Do not market it as an inflation hedge.
2. **Accessibility vs custody:** self-custody is hard for vulnerable users; custodial services require safeguarding, incident response and regulation.
3. **Community benefit vs centralization:** current KHAN prototype allows governance-approved mint, freeze, seizure and emergency pause. These powers must be disclosed and independently reviewed.
4. **Governance vs accountability:** 3-of-5 multisig reduces single-key risk but can still collude; seizure escrow lacks independent arbitration.
5. **Cross-border use vs law:** licensing, sanctions, AML/CFT, data privacy, tax and consumer-protection requirements vary by jurisdiction.
6. **Research ethics:** never test on vulnerable communities with real money without appropriate approvals, safety controls and informed consent.

## Evidence and methods

- **World Bank WDI**, inflation consumer prices (annual %), `FP.CPI.TOTL.ZG`: https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/FP.CPI.TOTL.ZG (accessed October 2026; values and latest available years transcribed from country listing).
- **IMF World Economic Outlook database**, global and country macroeconomic outlook: https://data.imf.org/datasets/WEO (accessed October 2026).
- **IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2025**, background on slowing global growth and inflation uncertainty: https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WEO/Issues/2025/10/14/world-economic-outlook-october-2025
- This document is a **scoping review**; it is not a systematic literature review. Inflation observations are not sufficient to quantify payment exclusion, household welfare or demand for cryptocurrency.

## Next research milestones

1. Record country-year series, data release timestamps and methodological caveats in a versioned dataset.
2. Add evidence for remittance costs, account ownership, mobile money usage, internet availability and merchant acceptance (World Bank Global Findex, Remittance Prices Worldwide, ITU and national regulators).
3. Conduct separate interviews with households, merchants, civil-society organizations and compliance experts in candidate jurisdictions.
4. Benchmark an open-source simulation against cash, domestic instant payments, mobile money and regulated digital-money options.
5. Publish failure criteria: stop or redesign if costs, fraud, consumer harm, volatility or legal exposure exceed alternatives.
6. Obtain independent legal and security reviews before any real-value use.
